Pigmentation

CLINICAL OVERVIEW

Understanding

More even-looking skin. Balanced tone. Renewed confidence.

Pigmentation refers to areas of the skin that appear darker than the surrounding skin. It may appear as brown spots, dark patches, freckles, or uneven skin tone. This darkening occurs when there is an excess of melanin, the brown pigment that produces normal skin colour, forms deposits in the skin.

These changes can occur due to sun exposure, hormonal changes, acne, or skin inflammation.

Discoloration can occur anywhere and in any formation on the face, but it typically forms in patches or freckle-type lesions on the cheeks, forehead, nose, upper lip, or chin.

At D’Zen Derma, we focus on understanding your pigmentation concerns, skin type, and possible causes. Based on your skin assessment, we suggest suitable options to improve the appearance of uneven skin tone while caring for your skin’s natural balance.

TYPES OF CONCERNS

Types of Pigmentation

Melasma

Melasma appears as brown or grey-brown patches, commonly on the cheeks, forehead, nose, and upper lip. It is often associated with hormonal changes, sun exposure and thyroid disorders.

Uneven Skin Tone

Patches lighter or darker than the surrounding skin rather than distinct spots, making the overall complexion look uneven. This may be linked to sun exposure, acne marks, pollution or hormonal changes.

Ageing Spots & Freckles

Age spots and freckles are small, flat dark areas on the skin. They vary in size and usually appear on areas exposed to the sun, such as the face, hands, shoulders, and arms.

Post-Acne Pigmentation

Acne can leave dark or brown marks even after breakouts have healed. These marks develop due to skin inflammation and may take time to fade.

Tanned Skin

Tanning can put off your entire facial regime, especially when you want to attend a big event and look your best. Knowing this, we’re always very prepared to treat tanning, especially in a tropical country like ours.

Dull Skin

Skin loses natural luminosity with accumulated sun damage, pollution exposure, dehydration, and a slowing renewal cycle, resulting in a lacklustre, tired appearance. Not pigmentation in the true sense, but treated with treatment stacking.

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

Extremely common in Indian skin, PIH occurs as a result of inflammation or injury to the skin, such as acne, burns, cuts, or insect bites. It leads to the darkening of the affected area after the initial inflammation has healed.

CLEAR ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about pigmentation and its treatment.

What is pigmentation?
+
Pigmentation refers to areas of the skin that appear darker than the surrounding skin. It may appear as dark spots, patches, freckles, or uneven skin tone.
What causes pigmentation?
+
Pigmentation may develop due to sun exposure, hormonal changes, acne, skin inflammation, or irritation. The cause can vary from person to person.
What are the common types of pigmentation?
+
Common types include melasma, sun spots, freckles, and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. These may appear as dark spots or patches on different areas of the skin.
Is pigmentation the same as acne scars?
+
No. Pigmentation affects the colour of the skin, while acne scars affect its texture. Dark marks after acne may be pigmentation rather than actual scars.
Can pigmentation appear on areas other than the face?
+
Yes. Pigmentation may also appear on the neck, hands, arms, shoulders, and other areas of the body.
Can sun exposure make pigmentation worse?
+
Yes. Sun exposure can increase melanin production and may make some types of pigmentation darker or more noticeable.
Which procedures may help improve pigmentation?
+
Depending on the type of pigmentation, options such as Chemical Peels, Q-Switched Laser, Medipeel, and suitable skin-brightening procedures may be considered after a skin assessment.
How many sessions may be needed?
+
The number of sessions depends on the type of pigmentation, its depth, and the procedure selected. Your doctor can suggest a suitable plan after assessing your skin.
How long does it take to see improvement?
+
The time varies depending on the type of pigmentation and the approach used. Some concerns may improve sooner, while others may need ongoing care.
Can pigmentation return after it improves?
+
Yes. Some types of pigmentation may return, especially when the factors causing them continue. Sun protection and suitable skincare may help manage the concern.
How can I prevent pigmentation from getting worse?
+
Using sunscreen, limiting direct sun exposure, following suitable skincare, and avoiding unnecessary skin irritation may help manage pigmentation.
